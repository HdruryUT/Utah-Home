How the scores and estimates work
The plan
Buy a dated but livable house, do mostly cosmetic updates while you live there, and sell after about 3 years. If you own and live in it for 2 of the 5 years before selling, up to $250,000 of profit is federal-tax-free (IRS Section 121). Sell sooner and the profit is taxable.
Where listings come from
A scan every morning reads Century21's public single-family pages, which carry the local MLS listings, for each Utah County city. It then opens each new listing to confirm its type, lot, year built, description and photos. Zillow, Homes.com, UtahRealEstate.com and KSL are searched as cross-checks. Always confirm details on the listing link.
What's filtered out
Townhomes, condos, twin homes, homes on lots under 0.05 acres, land leases, 55+ communities, auctions, fewer than 2 bedrooms, and anything over $395k.
Fit score
3-year profit (30), fits your budget (15), resale layout: 3+ bed and 2 bath sell best (15), price per sqft vs the city's going rate (15), condition (10: dated-but-livable is best, already-remodeled has little upside, heavy fixers are hard to live in), price (5), lot and garage (5), no HOA (3), time on market (2). Dealbreakers subtract points: cash offers only (−15), short sale (−6), under contract (−5), may not qualify for FHA (−4).
Value after fix-up
The higher of (a) size × the city's median closed $/sqft for homes under $500k, scaled for home size, plus 12% for updated condition, and (b) asking price + fix-up cost × a return multiple (1.6 cosmetic, 1.5 heavy, 1.0 already updated). Capped at 1.6× asking.
Fix-up budget
Assumes you do much of the cosmetic work yourself: already updated $10/sqft, dated $25/sqft (paint, floors, fixtures, kitchen refresh), heavy fixer $55/sqft. Hiring out everything costs roughly 40–60% more.
3-year profit
Value after fix-up, grown 3.5% a year for 3 years, minus price, fix-up, 3% buying closing costs and 6% selling costs. Mortgage interest isn't subtracted, since it roughly replaces the rent you'd pay anyway, but property tax and insurance are part of your monthly payment.
Offer to hit your goal
The highest price that still clears your profit goal, holding the home's value fixed. Use it as a negotiating target, not an appraisal.
Monthly payment
Principal and interest at the current 30-year rate, plus property tax (~0.46% of price for a primary residence), insurance and PMI (because the down payment is under 20%), plus any HOA. "Comfortable" and "tight" are measured against your own budget limits.
Treat these as estimates
These are ballpark figures to help you compare homes. Get an inspection, a contractor's bid and a lender pre-approval before relying on them. Photos and listing details belong to the listing brokers.
